## Ivan Atknin

Tech lead and software architect. Most of my work is high-load backends and blockchain
infrastructure — designing how services talk to each other and making sure the whole thing
holds up when real traffic shows up.

I mostly write Go and Python. Over the years that's meant exchange backends, payment and
settlement systems, on-chain workers, analytics pipelines, and all the unglamorous plumbing
around them — Kubernetes, deployments, observability, the monitoring that keeps you from
getting paged at night. I care about the parts that don't demo well but decide whether a
system survives.

The other half of my background is mathematical. I started in physics — X-ray diffraction and
crystallography at the Shubnikov Institute of Crystallography / Kurchatov Institute — writing
numerical simulations and the algorithms behind them, with a couple of peer-reviewed papers
along the way:

- *Double-Crystal Rocking Curve Simulation Using 2D Spectral Angular Diagrams of X-Ray Radiation* — Crystallography Reports, 2018 · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1134/S1063774518040041)
- *Simulation of Reciprocal Space Maps in the Triple Crystal X-Ray Diffraction Scheme* — JETP, 2019 · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1134/S1063776119120136)

That's left me comfortable at the math-heavy end of engineering — numerical methods, applied
statistics and machine learning — and I bring it into the data and analytics work I do now.
